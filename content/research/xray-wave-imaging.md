Conventional X-ray imaging only measures how much a sample absorbs. X rays are also waves: they are refracted and scattered, and these signals reveal what absorption cannot see. This is what we exploit.

## Three signals, three sensitivities

| Signal | What it senses |
|---|---|
| **Absorption** | Hard tissue |
| **Refraction** (phase contrast) | Soft tissue |
| **Scattering** (dark-field) | Structures much smaller than the pixel, down to about one hundredth of its size |

Together they give multiscale 3D images of complete, unlabelled biological samples, from the whole organ down to a single cell and its environment.

## Applications across scales

- **Virtual histology**, a 3D, non-destructive alternative to slicing and staining, for brain (including Alzheimer's disease), osteoarticular tissue, liver and human embryonic and fetal organs.
- **Joint disease.** In mouse models of osteoarthritis, phase-contrast micro-CT shows cartilage and bone structure that MRI and conventional micro-CT miss. At clinically compatible scale, we studied the benefit of the technique for diagnosing osteoarthritis on human wrists, using synchrotron X rays at high energy.
- **Lung function.** With 4D synchrotron micro-tomography we map lung tissue deformation in vivo with microscopic resolution, and made the first observation of terminal bronchi closing during mechanical ventilation.
- **Contrast agents.** Spectral and dark-field imaging to track nanoparticle-labelled cells with small amounts of high-atomic-number or lower-atomic-number elements.

## From the synchrotron to the clinic

The synchrotron remains the gold standard. Our goal is to bring its image quality to conventional sources, then to patients.

1. **Synchrotron.** Where the methods are first demonstrated.
2. **Laboratory.** The [MUSITOX platform](../../platforms/musitox/), the world's first wave-based imaging platform using conventional X-ray sources. We simplified dark-field imaging down to a single, easy-to-make membrane that modulates the beam, with random or designed patterns.
3. **Clinic.** Phase-contrast and dark-field CT for oral healthcare, developed in the ANR ROOTS project and by the spin-off project arXimed, supported by the SATT Linksium.

{{< callout title="Oral healthcare" >}}
Compared with conventional cone-beam CT, phase-contrast CT of a jaw specimen shows teeth, gingiva, neurovascular bundles and even skin layers that conventional CT blurs. The arXimed project won the Grand Prix of the i-PhD innovation contest.
{{< /callout >}}
